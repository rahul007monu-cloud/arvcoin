How the price is built

There is one formula, and it is the whole product. No committee sets the price, no operator adjusts it, and money arriving or leaving does not move it.

ARV(t) = ₹1 × BTC(t) ÷ BTC(launch)
Bitcoin at launch was — ($93,347.59 at USD/INR 85.60). Both figures are locked in arv-config.js and never change.

Deposits cannot lift it

ARV's price is a function of market data alone — it never reads how many units exist or how much money is held. New deposits issue new units at the current price rather than bidding it up, so an early holder gains nothing from a later one arriving. That is the difference between an index unit and a number someone can push.

Quoted in rupees, on purpose

You deposit rupees and the treasury holds Bitcoin, so ARV is quoted in rupees — which means its printed change is what your money actually did, currency movement included. Quote it in dollars instead and the headline percentage would stop matching your statement.

Redemption is priced, not negotiated

There is no order book and no counterparty. Units are issued and redeemed against the index price, so there is no spread to cross and no queue to wait in — but equally, no one to sell to at a premium. The price is the price.

The 30% is not deducted from your payout. Only the exit fee, GST and 1% TDS are withheld at redemption. The 30% plus 4% cess on your gain is your own liability, paid when you file — the TDS already withheld counts against it. Losses on virtual digital assets cannot be set off against gains or carried forward, so two trades that cancel out can still leave tax owing. Every figure is itemised on the redeem screen and again in your tax statement.
