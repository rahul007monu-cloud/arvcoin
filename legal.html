Risk disclosure

You can lose money. ARV tracks Bitcoin. Bitcoin has repeatedly fallen by more than half from a peak and taken years to recover. A holding worth ₹1,00,000 today can be worth ₹40,000 with no warning, no floor and no recourse. Only commit money you can afford to lose entirely.

Specific risks

  • Market risk. ARV's price is Bitcoin's percentage move applied to ₹1. There is no cap on the downside short of zero, and no mechanism that arrests a fall.
  • Currency risk. ARV is quoted in rupees while Bitcoin trades in dollars, so the USD/INR rate is part of your return. A strengthening rupee reduces your rupee gain even when Bitcoin rises.
  • No guaranteed return. Nothing on this platform is a promise, projection or assurance of any return. Past performance — including any chart shown here — says nothing about future movement.
  • Liquidity and settlement risk. Redemption is against the index price, not a market maker. Payouts are processed within the stated window but are not instantaneous, and the price can move between your request and settlement.
  • Tracking risk. The treasury holds the underlying asset. Any divergence between what is held and what is owed — from execution slippage, timing or operational error — is borne by holders.
  • Counterparty and custody risk. The underlying asset is held with third-party exchanges or custodians. Their failure, freeze or compromise affects your holding.
  • Tax risk. Gains are taxed at a flat rate with no deduction for costs and no set-off of losses. Two redemptions that cancel out economically can still leave tax owing. See tax treatment.
  • Regulatory risk. Virtual digital assets are not regulated as securities in India and are not covered by any investor protection or compensation scheme. The legal and tax framework can change with a budget.
  • Technology and data risk. Prices come from third-party exchange feeds. An outage, a regional block or bad data can interrupt pricing, charts or the ability to transact.

What it is not

  • Not a deposit. There is no interest, no assured return and no capital protection.
  • Not a security. It confers no ownership of the operating entity, no dividend and no voting right.
  • Not backed by a promise. Its value derives from the underlying asset held against it, not from an undertaking by the operator.
  • Not traded on a secondary market. Units are issued and redeemed against the index price. There is no order book, no bid/ask spread and no counterparty, and units are not transferable between users.
  • Not affected by other holders. Deposits and redemptions do not move the price. Nobody's entry improves or damages anybody else's position.

Tax treatment

Two different things, and they are not interchangeable. TDS is withheld by this platform at redemption. The 30% plus cess is not withheld — it is your own liability at filing time.

What this means in practice

  • Only cost of acquisition is deductible. Entry fees, exit fees, GST and any other expense do not reduce your taxable gain.
  • Losses cannot be set off. Not against other virtual digital asset gains, not against other income, and not carried forward to a later year. Gaining ₹1,00,000 on one redemption and losing ₹1,00,000 on another leaves you flat but still owing tax on ₹1,00,000.
  • The rate is flat. 30% regardless of your slab, and regardless of how long you held.
  • Holding is not a taxable event. Tax arises on transfer. An unrealised gain, however large, is not taxed until you redeem.
  • Cost basis is FIFO. The oldest units you hold are treated as redeemed first.
  • TDS is a credit, not an extra tax. It is set against your liability and appears in Form 26AS and AIS. Where it exceeds the liability, the excess is refundable when you file.

Your tax statement computes all of this from your recorded transactions and exports it as CSV. It is a computation, not a filing, and not tax advice — have a chartered accountant review it.

Privacy

What is collected

  • Email address and password (stored hashed, never in plain text).
  • Name and PAN — required to apply the correct TDS rate and to report it.
  • Your payout UPI ID.
  • Transaction records: amounts, units, prices, timestamps, references.

What is not collected

  • No advertising or cross-site tracking, and no third-party analytics.
  • Card details are never handled — payment is by UPI, directly between your bank app and the platform's account.

Where it lives

Account and transaction data sit in a Postgres database with row-level security: a signed-in browser can read only its own rows, and cannot write holdings, transactions or tax records at all. Those are written solely by server-side functions.

Retention and deletion

Transaction and tax records are retained as long as required for tax and anti-money-laundering purposes, which is generally several years beyond the relevant financial year, and cannot be deleted on request during that period. Profile data can be corrected at any time and deleted once no holdings and no retention obligation remain. Write to the address under grievance redressal.

Third parties

Market prices are fetched from public exchange APIs. Those requests carry no account information — only which instrument and timeframe is being requested.

Redemption and refunds

  • There is no cooling-off period. Once units are issued you hold market exposure. Changing your mind means redeeming at the price then prevailing, which may be lower than you paid.
  • Fees already charged are not refunded on redemption. They pay for execution that has already happened.
  • Payments that cannot be matched are returned. A deposit without a usable reference, or from an account not belonging to you, is refunded to the source and no units are issued.
  • Failed payouts. If a payout is rejected by your bank — usually a wrong or closed UPI ID — the amount is held and retried once you correct the ID. It is not converted back into units.
  • Operational error. Where units are issued or redeemed at a demonstrably wrong price because of an error on this side, the position is corrected by a compensating ledger entry. The original entry is not deleted.

Regulatory status

Read this before you commit money. Virtual digital assets in India are taxed but are not regulated as securities. This platform is not registered with SEBI, is not a mutual fund or collective investment scheme, is not an RBI-regulated deposit taker, and is not covered by any investor protection, insurance or compensation scheme. If it fails, there is no statutory fallback.

Pooling money from the public to invest on their behalf, and holding customer funds, engage a number of Indian regimes — among them registration with the Financial Intelligence Unit as a reporting entity, obligations under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, the Banning of Unregulated Deposit Schemes Act, and the collective investment scheme provisions of the SEBI Act. Which of these apply depends on how the operation is actually structured, and the answer is a matter for qualified counsel rather than for software.

The operator must complete that analysis, obtain whatever registrations it concludes are required, and have a securities lawyer or company secretary review every page of this document before the platform is offered to the public.
